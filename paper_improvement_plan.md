# Paper Improvement Plan: Making the Coherence-Depth Claims Reviewer-Proof

This plan turns three high-level recommendations into concrete paper revisions, theorem statements, proof sketches, mechanization tasks, and acceptance criteria.

The guiding editorial principle is:

> Keep the ambitious depth-two thesis, but make the proof boundary exact: prove a precise theorem for actual CCHM/Cubical Agda open-box structures, prove at least one nontrivial surface-to-`C_ext` adequacy theorem, and make the mechanization table distinguish postulate-free model checking from semantic transfer.

The most important technical warning is this:

> Do **not** claim that arbitrary higher filler spaces in cubical type theory are contractible. The theorem should concern the specific **structural horn-extension package** generated by the sealing grammar: an open-box extension problem with one face left free, not a closed-boundary filler problem.

References used for the target formulation:

- CCHM cubical type theory: https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02108
- Agda cubical-mode documentation: https://agda.readthedocs.io/en/latest/language/cubical.html
- Cubical Agda primitives: https://agda.github.io/cubical/Cubical.Core.Primitives.html
- Cubical Agda paper: https://staff.math.su.se/anders.mortberg/papers/cubicalagda.pdf
- Current artifact/trust-boundary repository: https://github.com/efficient-novelty/coherence-depth

## 0. Executive summary of the revision

### Current vulnerability

The paper's current horn argument is conceptually plausible, but too much is hidden behind phrases such as "standard open-box contractibility" or "hcomp computes the missing face." A skeptical reader can object that:

1. The space of fillers for a **closed boundary** is not generally contractible.
2. CCHM and Cubical Agda use precise notions of cofibration, partial element, and composition; the paper's `H_k(partial)` needs to be identified with those, not just described diagrammatically.
3. The Agda artifact may be postulate-free while still checking a synthetic datatype whose constructors already encode the desired result.
4. The adequacy bridge is currently too abstract: it should be demonstrated on one real surface calculus.

### Target revision

Add three new pieces.

1. **A precise open-box theorem.** Define the structural horn-extension object using actual CCHM/Cubical Agda partial elements and subtypes, then prove it contractible, naturally in substitution. This replaces the current informal Lemma 5.17.
2. **A nontrivial adequacy instance.** Introduce a small surface calculus `SMod` for a global modality over a small Tarski universe with function codes. Prove elaboration into `C_ext` preserves well-typedness, primitive/derived trace classification, historical support, and `mu`.
3. **A sharper mechanization section.** Separate: postulate-free checking of the fixed model, mechanized adequacy for the small surface calculus, semantic transfer assumptions for general Cubical Agda/CCHM systems, and paper-level interpretations.

### Proposed new claim hierarchy

Use this hierarchy consistently.

| Claim type | What may be claimed | What must not be implied |
|---|---|---|
| Internal model theorem | Depth two holds for the fixed `C_ext` calculus and its structural horn-extension discipline. | Arbitrary cubical foundations automatically satisfy the discipline. |
| Actual cubical horn theorem | The specific open-box extension package used by the paper is contractible and substitution-stable in CCHM/Cubical Agda form. | Every closed higher filler type is contractible. |
| Adequacy instance | A concrete small surface calculus elaborates into `C_ext` preserving trace tags and `mu`. | Arbitrary Cubical Agda programs elaborate into `C_ext`. |
| Mechanization evidence | Agda checks the internal theorem and the stated small adequacy instance without local postulates. | Postulate-free code alone proves the broad semantic thesis. |

## 1. Replacement for the horn argument

### 1.1. Paper-level diagnosis

The current paper says, in effect:

> A marginal depth-`k >= 3` structural obligation is a horn-extension object `H_k(partial) = Sigma gamma . Fill_partial(gamma)`. Cubical Kan operations compute the missing face and filler, so this factor is contractible.

This is the right high-level shape, but the revision should make four distinctions explicit.

1. `H_k(partial)` is not a closed-boundary filler space. It is the **total extension space of an open box**, where the missing face is part of the unknown.
2. The cofibration/partial-boundary object must be the one used by the cubical system: CCHM face formulas or Cubical Agda's `Partial phi A`/`Sub A phi u` discipline.
3. Contractibility must be a theorem about this object, not a property smuggled in by defining `H_k` as a singleton record.
4. The construction must be natural in substitution, because sealing and normalization occur under contexts, telescope maps, and historical-support substitutions.

### 1.2. Replace Lemma 5.17 with a precise theorem

Add a subsection before the current exact-stabilization proof:

> **5.4.1. Actual cubical open-box extension theorem**

Then replace the old Lemma 5.17 with the following statement.

#### Theorem H1: Structural open-box extension is contractible

Let `Gamma` be a cubical context. Let `A : Gamma, t : I |- Type_l` be a fibrant family. Let `phi` be a cofibration in `Gamma, t : I` describing the visible faces of a structural integration horn generated by Definition 5.12. In a CCHM presentation, `phi` is a face-lattice formula. In a Cubical Agda presentation, it is represented by an interval expression whose truth predicate is `IsOne phi`, with visible boundary data represented by `Partial phi (A t)`.

Assume compatible open-box data:

```text
u    : (t : I) -> Partial phi (A t)
u0   : A i0 [ phi |_{t=i0} -> u i0 ]
```

Here `A i0 [ phi -> u i0 ]` is the cubical subtype of elements of `A i0` agreeing with the visible boundary on `phi`.

Define the structural horn-extension object:

```text
OpenExt_Gamma(A, phi, u, u0)
  := Sigma (lid : A i1 [ phi |_{t=i1} -> u i1 ]) .
     Fill(A, phi, u, u0, lid)
```

where `Fill(A, phi, u, u0, lid)` is the type of relative fillers:

```text
fill : (t : I) -> A t

such that
  fill i0 = outS u0,
  fill i1 = outS lid,
  fill t agrees with u t on phi.
```

Then:

```text
Gamma |- isContr(OpenExt_Gamma(A, phi, u, u0)).
```

A canonical center is:

```text
center(A, phi, u, u0)
  := (canonical-lid, canonical-fill)

canonical-lid  := inS (comp A phi u u0)
canonical-fill := hfill A phi u u0
```

In Cubical Agda notation, `comp` may be defined from `hcomp` and `transp`; `hfill` may be defined from `hcomp` as in the Cubical Agda paper.

#### Naturality clause

For every cubical substitution `sigma : Delta -> Gamma`, there is a canonical equivalence:

```text
OpenExt_Gamma(A, phi, u, u0)[sigma]
  ~= OpenExt_Delta(A[sigma], phi[sigma], u[sigma], u0[sigma])
```

and this equivalence maps:

```text
center(A, phi, u, u0)[sigma]
```

to

```text
center(A[sigma], phi[sigma], u[sigma], u0[sigma]).
```

Moreover, the contraction paths commute with substitution propositionally; if the chosen cubical theory has judgmental uniformity of composition under substitutions, record the stronger judgmental form where available.

### 1.3. Important qualification to include immediately after H1

Add this boxed paragraph after the theorem statement.

> This theorem is an open-box theorem. It does not say that the type of fillers for a closed boundary, such as a full sphere boundary, is contractible. For a closed boundary the corresponding filler fiber can carry genuine homotopy. The contractibility used here comes from the fact that the missing face is part of the extension data. Equivalently, the horn inclusion is an anodyne/open-box inclusion, not a boundary inclusion.

This paragraph preempts the strongest likely reviewer objection.

## 1.4. Detailed proof sketch for Theorem H1

The proof should be written at two levels: a semantic CCHM proof and a Cubical Agda proof-term sketch.

### Step 1: Define the visible boundary as a real cofibration

For a depth-`k` structural integration obligation, introduce dimension variables:

```text
i_1, ..., i_{k-1}, t : I
```

Use `t` as the filling direction. The visible boundary formula `phi_k` is the disjunction of all face conditions generated by the sealing grammar except the missing remote face. Schematically:

```text
phi_k := visible-face_1 OR ... OR visible-face_m
```

In CCHM form, each visible face is a face-lattice equation such as `(i_j = 0)`, `(i_j = 1)`, or a transport-degenerate face forced by earlier public trace. In Cubical Agda form, each visible face is encoded by an interval expression, and the partial element is typed over `Partial phi_k`.

The key proof obligation is not contractibility yet. It is **boundary well-formedness**:

```text
Gamma, t : I |- u t : Partial phi_k (A t)
Gamma |- u0 : A i0 [ phi_k[t := i0] -> u i0 ]
```

All overlap compatibility equations among visible faces are absorbed by the `Partial`/`Sub` typing discipline. The paper should say this explicitly: once the boundary is encoded as a cubical partial element, overlap coherence is no longer a separate informal side condition.

### Step 2: Identify the paper's `Sigma gamma . Fill(gamma)` with `OpenExt`

The old notation is:

```text
H_k(partial) = Sigma (gamma : Gamma_miss(partial)) . Fill_partial(gamma)
```

Define an isomorphism:

```text
missing-face/filler package ~= open-box extension package
```

The missing face `gamma` is the restriction of the lid/filler to the previously omitted remote face. Conversely, given `gamma` plus a filler satisfying all faces, one obtains a lid agreeing with the visible boundary at `t=i1`.

In the paper, state this as a lemma.

#### Lemma H0: Missing-face packages are open-box extensions

For every structural horn generated by Definition 5.12, there is a canonical equivalence:

```text
H_k(partial) ~= OpenExt_Gamma(A_k, phi_k, u_k, u0_k).
```

This equivalence is natural in substitutions.

Proof idea:

1. Expand `Gamma_miss(partial)` as the missing `t=i1` face of the open box.
2. Expand `Fill_partial(gamma)` as a term over the whole cube agreeing with visible faces and with `gamma` on the missing face.
3. Reassociate the dependent sum: a lid plus a filler is the same as a missing face plus a filler, because the lid determines the missing face by restriction and the filler determines the lid at `t=i1`.
4. Naturality follows because restriction to faces and reassociation of dependent sums commute with substitution.

This lemma is where the paper connects its existing notation to actual cubical partial-element syntax.

### Step 3: Construct the center using cubical composition/filling

Use the cubical operations to define:

```text
canonical-lid : A i1 [ phi_k[t := i1] -> u_k i1 ]
canonical-fill : Fill(A_k, phi_k, u_k, u0_k, canonical-lid)
```

In Cubical Agda style:

```agda
canonicalLid : A i1 [ phi ↦ u i1 ]
canonicalLid = inS (comp A u u0)

canonicalFill : (t : I) -> A t
canonicalFill t = hfill A u u0 t
```

The exact Agda signature will depend on the chosen wrapper around `Partial`, but the paper should show enough pseudocode that a reader can see which primitive is used.

The proof obligations are:

```text
canonicalFill i0 = outS u0
canonicalFill i1 = outS canonicalLid
canonicalFill t agrees with u t on phi
```

These are precisely the computation/stability equations for `hfill` and `comp`/`hcomp`.

### Step 4: Prove contraction of any other extension to the canonical one

Let:

```text
e = (lid, fill) : OpenExt_Gamma(A, phi, u, u0).
```

We need a path:

```text
contract(e) : Path OpenExt e center.
```

There are two ways to present the proof. The paper should give both, but formalize one.

#### Proof route A: Contractibility of total path/extension spaces

Topological intuition: the horn inclusion into the cube is an anodyne open-box inclusion. The type of extensions of a fixed horn map over that inclusion is contractible. The canonical filler selected by the algebraic Kan operation is a chosen center.

Type-theoretic implementation:

1. View `OpenExt` as a relative extension type:

   ```text
   Sigma (f : Cube -> A) . f|Horn = boundary
   ```

2. Use cubical connections to define a relative homotopy that deforms any extension `f` to the canonical extension `hfill` while keeping the horn boundary fixed.
3. This homotopy is itself obtained by one higher-dimensional composition in the function/extension type.
4. Since the homotopy fixes the visible boundary, it descends to a path in `OpenExt`.

This is conceptually clean and reviewer-friendly.

#### Proof route B: Direct Cubical Agda path in the sigma type

Construct a path in the sigma type by an extra interval variable `j`:

```text
contract e j : OpenExt
```

with:

```text
contract e i0 = e
contract e i1 = center
```

The lid component at `j` is defined by a composition in `A i1` whose boundary is:

```text
j = i0  -> outS lid
j = i1  -> outS canonical-lid
phi     -> u i1
```

The filler component is defined by a composition in the type of relative fillers, with boundary:

```text
j = i0  -> fill
j = i1  -> canonical-fill
t = i0  -> outS u0
t = i1  -> lid-path j
phi     -> u t
```

All boundary overlaps are forced by the assumptions that both `fill` and `canonical-fill` are relative fillers for the same visible boundary.

The final path in `OpenExt` is assembled by sigma-path formation.

This direct route is closer to what an Agda proof would look like. If it becomes too cumbersome in Agda, formalize a wrapper theorem for relative extension types and prove it once.

### Step 5: Prove substitution naturality

State a separate lemma.

#### Lemma H2: Substitution stability of structural open boxes

For any substitution `sigma : Delta -> Gamma`:

```text
(phi_k, u_k, u0_k)[sigma]
```

is again the structural open-box data generated by the same sealing rule applied in context `Delta`.

Then prove:

```text
center_Gamma(A, phi, u, u0)[sigma]
  = center_Delta(A[sigma], phi[sigma], u[sigma], u0[sigma])
```

and:

```text
contract_Gamma(e)[sigma]
  = contract_Delta(e[sigma]).
```

Proof idea:

1. Substitution acts structurally on interval expressions, face formulas, partial elements, and subtypes.
2. CCHM composition is uniform under substitutions by definition of the cubical CwF/composition structure.
3. Cubical Agda primitives are stable under substitutions in the type checker; for the paper, record this as a lemma about the chosen primitive wrapper rather than as an unproved slogan.
4. Reassociation of sigma types and restriction to faces commute with substitution.

This naturality lemma is essential. Without it, the horn theorem may prove pointwise contractibility but not adequacy under elaboration/normalization.

### Step 6: Use H1 to repair exact stabilization

After H1, the existing proof of exact stabilization can become very short and much stronger.

Replace the current proof of Theorem 5.18 with this structure:

1. By structural horn representation, for `k >= 3`:

   ```text
   Real_k(X) ~= Sigma (partial : Real_{k-1}(X)) . H_k(partial)
   ```

2. By Lemma H0:

   ```text
   H_k(partial) ~= OpenExt(A_k, phi_k, u_k, u0_k)
   ```

3. By Theorem H1, each fiber is contractible, naturally in `partial`.
4. Therefore the projection:

   ```text
   Real_k(X) -> Real_{k-1}(X)
   ```

   is an equivalence.
5. Iterate from `k` down to `2`.

This proof no longer depends on an ambiguous phrase like "standard open-box contractibility".

## 1.5. Concrete paper edits for the horn section

### Replace Definition 5.12 with a two-layer definition

Current Definition 5.12 mixes the intuitive geometry and the formal package. Split it.

#### Definition 5.12a: Structural horn shape

A structural horn shape is the data generated by the sealing grammar:

```text
(A_k, phi_k, u_k, u0_k)
```

where:

- `A_k` is the type family in which the integration cell lives;
- `phi_k` is the visible-face cofibration;
- `u_k` is the visible partial boundary;
- `u0_k` is the base face, compatible with `u_k`.

#### Definition 5.12b: Structural horn-extension object

The associated exact marginal obligation is:

```text
H_k(partial) := OpenExt(A_k, phi_k, u_k, u0_k).
```

Then add a remark:

> The older notation `Sigma gamma . Fill(gamma)` is retained as an intuitive decomposition into missing face plus filler. Formally, the object used in the proof is `OpenExt`, and Lemma H0 identifies the two.

### Add a negative example

Add a short warning example after H1:

```text
BoundaryFill(A, full-boundary) := fillers of a closed square/sphere boundary in A.
```

This type is not generally contractible. For example, in a type with nontrivial loops, closed 2-dimensional boundaries can detect nontrivial higher paths. The paper does not use such a type.

This negative example is important because it shows the authors know the standard objection.

### Add an implementation note

Add a paragraph:

> In the mechanization, the theorem should not be implemented by defining `H_k` as a singleton record. The implementation should expose the open-box data as `Partial`/`Sub` data, define the canonical center using `hcomp`/`transp`/`hfill`, and prove contractibility of that exposed extension type.

## 1.6. Agda deliverables for the horn theorem

Create a new module group rather than overloading the existing `KanSubsumption.agda`.

```text
agda/CubicalOpenBox/Base.agda
agda/CubicalOpenBox/Extension.agda

```

### `CubicalOpenBox/Base.agda`

Defines wrappers around actual cubical primitives:

```agda
record OpenBox (A : I -> Type ell) : Type (lsuc ell) where
  field
    phi : I
    u   : (i : I) -> Partial phi (A i)
    u0  : A i0 [ phi ↦ u i0 ]
```

The exact syntax may vary with the library version, but it should use real `Partial` and `Sub`/`_[_↦_]`.

### `CubicalOpenBox/Extension.agda`

Defines:

```agda
record Filler (B : OpenBox A) (lid : A i1 [ phi ↦ u i1 ]) : Type ell where
  field
    fill : (i : I) -> A i
    fill-i0 : fill i0 ≡ outS u0
    fill-i1 : fill i1 ≡ outS lid
    fill-phi : ... -- agreement with u on phi

OpenExt : OpenBox A -> Type ell
OpenExt B = Sigma Lid (Filler B)
```

If the `Sub` type gives some equations judgmentally, use that to reduce proof burden. But keep enough structure visible that the theorem is not vacuous.

### `CubicalOpenBox/Contractible.agda`

Defines:

```agda
canonicalOpenExt : (B : OpenBox A) -> OpenExt B
openExtIsContr   : (B : OpenBox A) -> isContr (OpenExt B)
```

This is the central theorem. If proving `openExtIsContr` for full generality is too hard in Agda, state a precisely scoped version for the structural horn shapes actually generated by the sealing grammar. Do not silently weaken the theorem in prose.

### `CubicalOpenBox/Substitution.agda`

Defines the naturality API. Because Agda does not expose raw contexts/substitutions in the same way as a CwF paper proof, use one of these two strategies.

**Strategy A: semantic CwF record.** Define a small record for substitutions, reindexing, partial elements, and composition uniformity. Prove naturality in that record.

**Strategy B: parametric modules.** Express naturality as stability under explicitly passed parameters and renamings used by the structural grammar. This is less general but still useful.

The paper should be honest about which strategy is used.

### `Metatheory/StructuralHornToOpenBox.agda`

Shows that every structural horn generated by the sealing grammar yields an `OpenBox`.

Key theorem names:

```agda
structuralHornToOpenBox
structuralHornPackageIsoOpenExt
structuralHornExtensionIsContr
structuralHornExtensionNaturality
```

### Required smoke tests

Add tests that instantiate the theorem on the paper's running example.

```text
Test/CubicalOpenBox/FlatArrHorn.agda
Test/CubicalOpenBox/NaturalitySmoke.agda
Test/CubicalOpenBox/ClosedBoundaryNotClaimed.agda
```

The `ClosedBoundaryNotClaimed` test need not prove noncontractibility; it can simply ensure closed-boundary filler types are not routed through `openExtIsContr`.

## 2. Nontrivial adequacy instance: a small surface calculus for a global modality

### 2.1. Why this is needed

The current adequacy discussion says what an adequacy theorem would need to preserve, but it does not show the bridge on a realistic surface language. A reviewer can therefore say:

> The model theorem is internally consistent, but maybe no nontrivial surface calculus actually elaborates into it while preserving `mu` and primitive/derived trace.

A single worked adequacy instance would change the paper's evidential status. It would show that `C_ext` is not just a tailor-made accounting object; it receives a meaningful surface syntax.

### 2.2. Chosen instance: `SMod`, a global modality over a small Tarski universe

Use a deliberately small calculus. It should be expressive enough to generate unary trace, binary trace, and one derived horn, but small enough to formalize fully.

#### Old interface

The old sealed layer exports a small Tarski universe with function codes:

```text
record OldU where
  U      : Type_1
  El     : U -> Type
  Arr    : U -> U -> U
  ElArr  : (A B : U) -> Path Type (El (Arr A B)) (El A -> El B)
```

Treat `OldU` as an opaque public interface. The new surface extension can refer only to these exported fields, not to hidden derivations inside the old layer.

#### New payload

The surface declaration introduces a global modality-like operation:

```text
payload
  Flat : Type -> Type
  eta  : (A : Type) -> A -> Flat A
```

This is payload, not trace.

#### Primitive structural trace

The declaration must specify how `Flat` integrates with the old universe interface:

```text
trace
  FlatU : U -> U

  flat-El : (A : U) ->
    Path Type (El (FlatU A)) (Flat (El A))

  flat-code-Arr : (A B : U) ->
    Path U (FlatU (Arr A B)) (Arr (FlatU A) (FlatU B))

  flat-Arr : (A B : U) ->
    Path Type (Flat (El A -> El B))
              (Flat (El A) -> Flat (El B))
```

The exact classification can be adjusted to the paper's basis convention, but the recommended schema-level reading is:

- `FlatU`/`flat-El` are unary action/decoding trace against the old universe interface.
- `flat-code-Arr` and `flat-Arr` are binary comparison trace expressing preservation of the old function-code/decoding structure.
- The compatibility among `flat-El`, `flat-code-Arr`, `flat-Arr`, and `ElArr` is a derived structural horn.

#### Generated derived horn

For each `A B : U`, there are two routes from:

```text
El (FlatU (Arr A B))
```

to:

```text
Flat (El A) -> Flat (El B)
```

Left route:

```text
El (FlatU (Arr A B))
  -- flat-El (Arr A B) -->
Flat (El (Arr A B))
  -- cong Flat (ElArr A B) -->
Flat (El A -> El B)
  -- flat-Arr A B -->
Flat (El A) -> Flat (El B)
```

Right route:

```text
El (FlatU (Arr A B))
  -- cong El (flat-code-Arr A B) -->
El (Arr (FlatU A) (FlatU B))
  -- ElArr (FlatU A) (FlatU B) -->
El (FlatU A) -> El (FlatU B)
  -- transport along flat-El A and flat-El B -->
Flat (El A) -> Flat (El B)
```

The surface calculus may allow the programmer to write a field:

```text
flat-Arr-coh : Path (Path Type ... ...) left-route right-route
```

but the adequacy theorem should classify it as **derived**, not primitive. If the programmer omits it, elaboration synthesizes it using the open-box theorem.

This makes the instance nontrivial: it has a real payload, primitive unary/binary trace, and a higher trace field that appears naturally but is eliminated from `mu`.

## 2.3. Surface syntax

Define a small grammar.

```text
Surface contexts:
  Gamma ::= empty | Gamma, x : T

Old interfaces:
  O ::= oldU(uName)

Extension declarations:
  E ::= modality M over O {
          payload Flat eta;
          action FlatU flat-El;
          comparison flat-code-Arr flat-Arr;
          optional derived flat-Arr-coh;
        }
```

Use two versions of the declaration form.

### Version 1: minimal surface declaration

```text
modality M over L : OldU {
  payload Flat eta;
  trace FlatU flat-El flat-code-Arr flat-Arr;
}
```

The horn is generated by elaboration.

### Version 2: redundant explicit horn declaration

```text
modality M over L : OldU {
  payload Flat eta;
  trace FlatU flat-El flat-code-Arr flat-Arr;
  derived flat-Arr-coh = <user term>;
}
```

The explicit `flat-Arr-coh` must be checked against the generated horn type but tagged as derived. It must not change `mu`.

Including both versions lets the paper prove presentation invariance in a concrete setting.

## 2.4. Surface typing judgments

Add a compact formal system with judgments:

```text
Gamma |- OldU(L)
Gamma |- Payload(P)
Gamma; L : OldU; P : Payload |- Trace(T)
Gamma; L; P; T |- DerivedHorn(H)
Gamma |- E : SModExtension(L)
```

The typing rules should be syntax-directed.

### Payload rule

```text
Gamma |- Flat : Type -> Type
Gamma |- eta  : (A : Type) -> A -> Flat A
------------------------------------------------
Gamma |- payload(Flat, eta)
```

### Trace rule

```text
Gamma |- FlatU : U -> U
Gamma |- flat-El : ...
Gamma |- flat-code-Arr : ...
Gamma |- flat-Arr : ...
------------------------------------------------
Gamma; L; payload |- trace(...)
```

### Derived horn rule

The derived horn type is computed from the trace fields:

```text
HornType(L, P, T, A, B)
  := Path (Path Type source target)
          (leftRoute L P T A B)
          (rightRoute L P T A B)
```

Then:

```text
Gamma; L; P; T |- flat-Arr-coh : (A B : U) -> HornType(L,P,T,A,B)
```

or, if omitted:

```text
Gamma; L; P; T |- synthHorn(L,P,T) : (A B : U) -> HornType(L,P,T,A,B)
```

where `synthHorn` is generated by the open-box theorem.

## 2.5. Elaboration into `C_ext`

Define a function:

```text
Elab : SModExtension -> RawExtension_Cext
```

For the minimal declaration:

```text
Elab(modality M over L { payload; trace })
  = rawExtension {
      payloadClauses = [Flat, eta];
      actionClauses = [FlatU, flat-El];
      comparisonClauses = [flat-code-Arr, flat-Arr];
      hornClauses = [generated flat-Arr-coh tagged derived];
      exportSelection = payload + primitive trace;
    }
```

For the redundant declaration with explicit horn:

```text
Elab(... derived flat-Arr-coh = h)
  = same rawExtension, but hornClauses contains h tagged derived
```

Normalization should replace either explicit or synthesized horn witnesses by the canonical derived horn field, so both declarations have the same normalized public signature and the same `mu`.

### Trace tag mapping

Add a table in the paper.

| Surface field | `C_ext` clause class | Primitive/derived | Counted by `mu`? | Support |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Flat` | payload | payload | no, counted in `kappa` | new layer |
| `eta` | payload | payload | no, counted in `kappa` | new layer |
| `FlatU` | action | primitive trace | yes | old `U` |
| `flat-El` | action/decoding comparison | primitive trace | yes, according to basis convention | old `El` |
| `flat-code-Arr` | comparison | primitive trace | yes | old `Arr` |

## 2.6. Adequacy theorem for `SMod`

Add a theorem in Section 3 or as a new Section 4 before the general metatheory.

### Theorem A1: Adequacy of the modal surface calculus

For every well-typed `SMod` declaration `E` over an old interface `L : OldU`:

1. **Elaboration soundness.** `Elab(E)` is a well-typed admissible raw extension declaration in `C_ext`.
2. **Payload preservation.** The payload part of `Elab(E)` is exactly the payload of `E`, up to the standard telescope isomorphisms used by presentation equivalence.
3. **Primitive trace preservation.** The primitive trace fields of `E` correspond bijectively to the primitive action/comparison clauses of `Elab(E)`.
4. **Derived horn preservation.** The surface horn `flat-Arr-coh`, whether explicit or synthesized, elaborates to a derived structural horn clause. It is not counted as primitive trace.
5. **Support preservation.** Historical support sets of primitive trace fields are preserved by elaboration.
6. **`mu` preservation.** The normalized surface cost equals the normalized `C_ext` cost:

   ```text
   mu_SMod(E) = mu_Cext(Elab(E)).
   ```

7. **Presentation invariance.** The minimal declaration and the redundant explicit-horn declaration are presentation-equivalent after elaboration:

   ```text
   Elab(E_min) ~= Elab(E_explicitHorn)
   ```

   and have the same normalized public signature.

### Proof sketch

#### Part 1: Elaboration soundness

Induct on the typing derivation of `E`.

- Payload fields map directly to raw payload clauses.
- `FlatU` and `flat-El` map to raw action clauses because their target types are exactly the public action targets required by the old interface `U`/`El`.
- `flat-code-Arr` and `flat-Arr` map to raw comparison clauses because they compare the two ways of applying the new modality to old function-code structure.
- The derived horn maps to the structural horn shape produced by the grammar for `Arr`/`ElArr` compatibility.

The only nontrivial typing check is the horn. It follows from Theorem H1 after constructing the visible boundary from `flat-El`, `flat-code-Arr`, `flat-Arr`, and `ElArr`.

#### Part 2: Payload and primitive trace preservation

Define maps:

```text
payloadFields(E) -> payloadClauses(Elab(E))
primitiveTraceFields(E) -> primitiveTraceClauses(Elab(E))
```

and prove they are bijections after telescope normalization. This is a finite structural proof, not a semantic cubical argument.

#### Part 3: Derived horn preservation

Show that `flat-Arr-coh` has the exact open-box extension type associated with the `Arr` compatibility horn.

- If the field is omitted, elaboration inserts the canonical center from Theorem H1 and tags it derived.
- If the field is explicit, type checking ensures it inhabits the same horn-extension type. Contractibility of the horn-extension type gives a path from the explicit witness to the canonical one, so replacing it is presentation-equivalent and does not change `mu`.

This is the strongest reason to use an open-box contractibility theorem rather than just a chosen filler.

#### Part 4: Support preservation

Compute support syntactically.

- `Flat` and `eta` have support in the new layer and are payload.
- `FlatU` and `flat-El` mention only `L.U` and `L.El` as old interface sites.
- `flat-code-Arr` and `flat-Arr` mention the old function-code/decoding sites.
- `flat-Arr-coh` mentions no new primitive support beyond the union of the lower trace fields; it is marked derived.

Therefore historical support of primitive fields is unchanged by elaboration.

#### Part 5: `mu` preservation

Let:

```text
N_surf(E)
```

be the normalized surface signature and:

```text
N_raw(Elab(E))
```

be the normalized `C_ext` signature.

Prove a canonical telescope isomorphism:

```text
N_surf(E) ~= N_raw(Elab(E))
```

that preserves primitive/derived tags. Then `mu` preservation follows immediately because `mu` is the cardinality of primitive trace schemas in the normalized signature.

#### Part 6: Explicit horn presentation invariance

For the declaration with an explicit horn witness `h`, use H1 to get:

```text
h = canonicalHorn
```

inside the horn-extension type. Therefore the presentation step:

```text
explicit derived horn field -> transparent canonical alias
```

is one of the allowed computational replacement/presentation-equivalence moves. Hence `mu` is unchanged.

## 5. Concrete theorem/proof text to insert

This section gives near drop-in text.

### 5.1. Drop-in theorem statement

> **Theorem (actual structural horn-extension contractibility).** Let `Gamma` be a cubical context and let `(A, phi, u, u0)` be a structural open box generated by the sealing grammar, where `A : Gamma, t : I |- Type_l`, `phi` is a visible-face cofibration, `u : (t : I) -> Partial phi (A t)` is the visible partial boundary, and `u0 : A i0 [ phi -> u i0 ]` is the base face. Define `OpenExt(A, phi, u, u0)` to be the type of a lid at `t=i1`, agreeing with `u i1` on `phi`, together with a filler from `u0` to that lid agreeing with `u` on `phi`. Then `OpenExt(A, phi, u, u0)` is contractible. Its center is the lid computed by cubical composition and the filler computed by cubical filling. The construction is natural in cubical substitutions.

### 5.2. Drop-in proof sketch

> **Proof sketch.** The visible structural boundary is first encoded as an actual cubical partial element. In the CCHM presentation, the visible faces form a face-lattice formula `phi`; in the Cubical Agda presentation, they form an interval expression whose truth predicate indexes `Partial phi`. The compatibility of overlaps among visible faces is exactly the well-typedness condition for the partial element `u`, and compatibility of the base with the visible boundary is the subtype judgment `u0 : A i0 [ phi -> u i0 ]`.
>
> The missing-face notation used earlier, `Sigma gamma . Fill(gamma)`, is canonically equivalent to the corresponding open-box extension type: a missing face plus a filler is the same as a lid plus a relative filler agreeing with the visible partial boundary. This equivalence is natural in substitution because restriction to faces, reassociation of dependent sums, and reindexing of partial elements are natural.
>
> Cubical composition gives the canonical lid, and cubical filling gives the canonical relative filler. For any other lid/filler pair, apply cubical composition one dimension higher in the relative extension type, with boundary given by the candidate filler on one side, the canonical filler on the other side, the original visible partial boundary on `phi`, and the base face at `t=i0`. The overlap equations are precisely the filler laws for the two endpoints. The result is a path in the extension type from the candidate pair to the canonical pair. This proves contractibility. Uniformity of cubical composition/filling under substitutions gives the naturality clause.
>
> This is not a contractibility theorem for closed-boundary filler spaces. The missing face is part of the extension data, and that is essential.

### 5.3. Drop-in adequacy theorem statement

> **Theorem (modal surface adequacy).** Let `E` be a well-typed declaration in the modal surface calculus `SMod` over an opaque old universe interface `OldU`. Then the elaboration `Elab(E)` is an admissible raw `C_ext` declaration. Elaboration preserves payload fields, primitive trace fields, historical support, and normalized public cost `mu`. The generated function-code compatibility horn elaborates to a derived structural horn clause; adding it explicitly as a surface field does not change the normalized public signature or `mu`.

### 5.4. Drop-in mechanization paragraph

> The mechanization table distinguishes four statuses. `checked-internal` means the claim is proved inside the fixed `C_ext` calculus. `checked-actual-cubical` means the claim is proved using the actual Cubical Agda partial-element/composition interface. `checked-adequacy-instance` means a specific surface calculus has a proved elaboration theorem into `C_ext`. `semantic-assumption` marks the broader transfer from arbitrary cubical surface languages or foundations. The absence of local postulates in a module is reported separately from these statuses: it is evidence that the encoded statement is checked by Agda, not by itself evidence that an external system has been adequately encoded.

## 8. Main risks and mitigations

### Risk 1: General `OpenExt` contractibility is hard to mechanize

Mitigation: prove it first for the structural horn shapes actually generated by the sealing grammar. The paper may state the general theorem as semantic CCHM mathematics and the structural instance as mechanized. Be explicit about this split.

Mitigation: formalize naturality in a small CwF-style semantic wrapper, or state it as a theorem about the semantic cubical foundation record. In Agda, add parametric smoke tests that cover the substitutions used by the structural grammar.

Mitigation: present `SMod` as a calculus of modality declarations that include this trace as an admissibility requirement. The theorem is about elaboration of well-typed declarations, not existence of such trace for every endofunctor `Flat`.
