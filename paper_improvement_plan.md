# Paper Improvement Plan: Coherence Depth, Sealed Extensions, and Fibonacci Scaling

This plan assumes the revised paper should preserve the ambitious scientific message while making a clean distinction between:

1. **Conjectural target claims** about cubical foundations in general; and
2. **Theorems actually established** for the fixed formal sealed-extension calculus and its mechanized trace discipline.

The central editorial move is to present the paper as a **model theorem plus evidence for a broader conjecture**, not as a finished transfer theorem for all cubical foundations.

---

## Executive summary

### Recommended new positioning

The paper should say:

> We conjecture that adequate sealed-extension calculi for cubical foundations have primitive public structural trace depth two, and that fully coupled foundational-core extension sequences therefore obey a two-step affine recurrence. We prove this conjectural picture for a fixed, formalized sealed-extension calculus with opaque public signatures, explicit payload/trace separation, and horn-computational replacement of higher structural obligations.

This is stronger rhetorically than simply weakening the paper. It lets the draft keep the big idea while making the proof boundary precise.

### What to demote to conjecture

Demote broad statements such as:

> Cubical foundations have coherence depth two.

and

> Cubical foundational extension cost scales by Fibonacci recurrence.

to conjectures unless they are explicitly scoped to the fixed raw calculus and adequacy package.

### What to claim as proved

Claim as proved:

> In the formal sealed-extension calculus defined in the paper, structural integration obligations above binary arity are represented as horn-extension packages. The calculus includes a horn-computational replacement discipline under which these higher packages are canonically derived from unary and binary public trace. Therefore the primitive public trace in minimal opaque signatures has depth at most two; the univalent swap obstruction shows depth one is insufficient; and under full coupling the resulting trace count satisfies the affine two-step recurrence.

### Main readability change

Move one concrete worked example to the front of the paper and use it throughout. The reader should see, before the abstract definitions, a single extension where:

- the **payload** is the genuinely new thing;
- the **unary trace** says how it acts on one old generator;
- the **binary trace** says it respects one old path/equivalence/comparison;
- the apparent **ternary obligation** is a horn package computed by cubical filling;
- the public signature counts only the primitive payload plus primitive unary/binary trace.

# Part A. Conjectures, theorem claims, and recommended wording

## A1. Claim hierarchy for the revised paper

Use a three-level hierarchy throughout the paper.

### Level 1: Broad conjectural program

These are the intended claims about cubical foundations and real foundational-core extension processes.

Example wording:

> The guiding conjecture is that cubical horn computation makes binary public trace sufficient for sealed structural integration: higher structural coherence obligations should be derivable from unary and binary boundary trace, while univalence prevents collapse to depth one.

This is the big idea. It should be stated boldly, but as a conjecture or programmatic thesis.

### Level 2: Fixed-calculus theorem package

These are the paper's actual formal contributions.

Example wording:

> We prove the guiding conjecture for the fixed sealed-extension calculus introduced here. In this calculus, obligations are generated by a structural-integration grammar, higher marginal obligations are represented as horn-extension packages, and normalized public signatures quotient away derived horn fillers.

This is the formal theorem object. It can be called a theorem without hedging.

### Level 3: Arithmetic consequence under full coupling

The Fibonacci recurrence is not a direct theorem of cubical type theory. It is a counting theorem once four ingredients are fixed:

1. primitive trace depth two;
2. a two-layer chronological window;
3. full coupling to the active primitive basis;
4. a constant-payload specialization.

Example wording:

> The Fibonacci law is the full-coupling envelope of the model, not a claim about ordinary library growth. Sparse or orthogonal extension sequences remain sparse.

## A2. Core conjectures to propose

## Conjecture 1: Cubical sealed-extension depth-two conjecture

### Suggested statement

> **Conjecture 1 (Cubical sealed-extension depth two).** Let `F` be an adequate cubical foundation with substitution-stable Kan composition and filling, univalence, and a sealing discipline for globally acting structural extensions. For every admissible sealed structural extension `X`, the exact structural integration obligations stabilize at binary trace: for all `k >= 2`, the realized obligation object at depth `k` is canonically equivalent to the realized obligation object at depth `2`, after quotienting by the foundation's specified structural horn-computation rules. Moreover, unary trace alone is insufficient in general.

### Why this conjecture is worth stating

This is the broad scientific thesis. It keeps the high-level phrase "cubical foundations have coherence depth two" but makes explicit that the claim depends on:

- a sealing discipline;
- a notion of structural integration obligation;
- adequate horn computation;
- univalence for the lower bound;
- a quotient/normalization discipline distinguishing primitive from derived public trace.

### What the paper proves toward it

The paper proves Conjecture 1 for the fixed sealed-extension calculus defined in Sections 2-5, not for every cubical foundation or every surface language.

### What remains open

A full proof of Conjecture 1 would require an adequacy/transfer theorem from a broad class of real cubical foundations or surface calculi into the paper's raw sealed-extension calculus. The current artifact can be evidence for such a transfer theorem, not the transfer theorem itself.

### Recommended placement

Put this conjecture in a new subsection near the end of the introduction:

> 1.4. Guiding conjectures and what is proved here

Then immediately follow it with the fixed-calculus theorem.

---

## Conjecture 2: Adequacy-transfer conjecture

### Suggested statement

> **Conjecture 2 (Adequacy transfer for cubical sealed extensions).** For a broad class of cubical foundations and surface extension languages, admissible sealed structural extensions admit a faithful interpretation in the raw sealed-extension calculus of this paper, preserving semantic admissibility, primitive-versus-derived trace status, historical support, and public trace cardinality.

### Why this conjecture is necessary

This is the missing bridge between the formal model and the broad slogan. Without it, the formal theorem says:

> Our calculus has depth two.

With it, the broader program could eventually say:

> Cubical foundations satisfying the adequacy hypotheses have depth two.

### What the paper proves toward it

The current paper proves a fixed raw bridge for the chosen calculus. It does not prove parser/elaborator adequacy for arbitrary Cubical Agda programs or arbitrary cubical foundations.

### Recommended framing

Do not hide this in a limitations section. Make it a positive research program:

> The present paper establishes the internal theorem and identifies the exact adequacy interface that a future transfer theorem must satisfy.

This makes the boundary look intentional rather than defensive.

---

## Conjecture 3: Canonicity/basis-density conjecture for globally acting extensions

### Suggested statement

> **Conjecture 3 (Global action forces active-basis coverage).** In a native foundational-core extension whose operational semantics advertises a total global action on the active interface, canonicity-preserving admissibility forces one primitive interaction clause for each active primitive basis site, up to canonical normalization and derived-field elimination.

### Why this should be conjectural or conditional

The current draft's theorem named "Global admissibility forces basis coverage" risks sounding like a semantic fact about all canonicity-preserving extensions. But the proof appears to depend on a bundled record of totality/single-clause coverage. In that case, the result is correct but conditional.

### Recommended rewording

Use one of these safer alternatives:

**Option A: Make it an assumption.**

> **Full-coupling coverage hypothesis.** A layer is fully coupled when its normalized public trace contains exactly one primitive interaction schema for every active primitive basis site.

Then the recurrence theorem becomes cleanly conditional.

**Option B: Make it a fixed-calculus theorem.**

> In the fixed calculus, the record defining native canonicity-preserving totality includes action on every primitive generator of the active basis. Therefore any layer satisfying that record has full active-basis coverage.

This is precise and avoids overclaiming that canonicity alone forces density in every system.

### What the paper proves toward it

The paper proves a record-level density theorem inside the fixed formal framework. Treat this as evidence for Conjecture 3, not as a general theorem about arbitrary operational semantics.

---

## Conjecture 4: Full-coupling Fibonacci envelope conjecture

### Suggested statement

> **Conjecture 4 (Fibonacci envelope for fully coupled cubical core growth).** In cubical foundational-core extension sequences satisfying depth-two public trace, two-layer chronological factorization, and full active-basis coupling, the primitive trace cost satisfies a two-step affine recurrence. Under constant payload size, the shifted sequence is Fibonacci.

### Why this conjecture matters

This is the headline scaling story. It should be presented as an envelope result, not a universal law of software-library growth.

### What the paper proves toward it

The paper proves the exact arithmetic recurrence in the fixed calculus once full coupling and constant payload are assumed. That is already a valuable theorem.

### Recommended wording for the paper

Use:

> The Fibonacci law is a full-coupling envelope theorem.

Avoid:

> Cubical libraries grow by Fibonacci recurrence.

---

## Conjecture 5: Recent-history Markov blanket conjecture

### Suggested statement

> **Conjecture 5 (Two-layer chronological Markov blanket).** In adequate cubical sealed-extension systems, every primitive public comparison with remote history factors through the normalized public signatures of the two most recent layers; deeper history contributes only derived horn data.

### Why it is useful

Depth as arity does not automatically imply a chronological window. This conjecture isolates the extra temporal claim needed for the recurrence theorem.

### Recommended use

State it only if the paper wants to emphasize the distinction between:

- cell/arity depth; and
- chronological dependency depth.

Otherwise fold it into Conjecture 1.

---

## Conjecture 6: Universality across horn-computational foundations

### Suggested statement

> **Conjecture 6 (Horn-computational universality).** The depth-two trace theorem is not specific to one CCHM presentation, but to any foundation whose structural integration obligations are generated as horn-extension packages with substitution-stable computable filling, plus a nontrivial univalent path structure for the binary lower bound.

### Why it is useful

This lets the paper discuss 2D foundations, CCHM-style cubical type theory, and related systems without implying that the current formalization covers all of them.

# A3. Specific claims the paper can safely say it proves

This section gives suggested theorem labels and wording. The exact theorem numbers can be adjusted to match the final draft.

## Proven Claim P1: Fixed sealed-extension calculus theorem

### Recommended statement

> **Theorem P1 (Depth-two public trace in the fixed sealed-extension calculus).** In the sealed-extension calculus defined in this paper, with opaque public signatures, normalized primitive/derived trace classes, and structural horn-extension obligations computed by the specified cubical Kan operations, primitive public structural trace has exact depth two.

### Important qualifier

This is not a theorem about arbitrary Cubical Agda programs or arbitrary cubical type theories. It is a theorem about the fixed calculus and its adequacy package.

### How it supports the conjectures

It is the main model theorem supporting Conjecture 1.

---

## Proven Claim P2: Exact stabilization of realized obligations

### Recommended statement

> **Theorem P2 (Exact stabilization of realized structural obligations).** For every admissible candidate `X` in the fixed calculus and every `k >= 2`, the realized structural obligation object `Real_k(X)` is canonically equivalent to `Real_2(X)`. The equivalence is obtained by decomposing the marginal depth-`k` contribution as a contractible structural horn-extension factor.

### Avoid saying

> All higher cubical fillers are contractible.

### Say instead

> The contractible factors are the structural horn-extension packages generated by the sealing calculus. Arbitrary internal higher filler spaces in cubical type theory are not claimed to be contractible.

---

## Proven Claim P3: Higher primitive trace elimination in minimal public signatures

### Recommended statement

> **Theorem P3 (Minimal-signature elimination).** In the fixed calculus, every admissible surface declaration is presentation-equivalent to a canonical normalized public signature whose primitive trace fields have historical arity at most two. Higher presented trace fields may occur in nonminimal presentations, but they are derived from lower trace and are not counted by `mu`.

### Why this matters

This separates exact obligation objects from public trace cost. It prevents reviewers from objecting that the paper silently conflates:

- an obligation type being present;
- an obligation being contractible;
- a field being transparently derived;
- a primitive public field being absent from a minimal signature.

The paper already recognizes this distinction; the revision should make it the central organizing device.

---

## Proven Claim P4: Binary lower bound from univalence

### Recommended statement

> **Theorem P4 (Binary lower bound).** In the univalent setting, unary objectwise action does not determine sealing in general. The two-point type together with the univalent path induced by swap yields a candidate whose unary clause is well-formed but whose binary naturality obligation fails. Hence depth one is insufficient.

### Strengthen if possible

The current swap example shows that a unary candidate may fail the binary check. To make the public-trace lower bound stronger, add a second version where two admissible extensions have identical unary public behavior but differ by a binary field not transparently reconstructible from unary data alone.

If that stronger version is not available, state the lower bound carefully:

> The swap obstruction proves that admissibility cannot be decided from unary trace alone.

Do not overstate it as:

> Every admissible extension must expose an independent binary public field.

unless the stronger witness is supplied.

---

## Proven Claim P5: Two-layer chronological window under factorization-complete export

### Recommended statement

> **Theorem P5 (Two-layer chronological window in the fixed calculus).** Assuming sealed layers export factorization-complete normalized trace data, every primitive comparison with remote history factors through the two most recent public signatures. Thus the depth-two arity bound becomes a genuine two-layer chronological window.

### Why this is important

The recurrence theorem needs a chronological window, not merely an arity bound. A binary obligation could, in principle, compare the new layer with a very old layer. The paper's recurrence requires the stronger claim that remote binary comparisons factor through recent exports.

### Recommended emphasis

Make this theorem a separate milestone in the introduction and theorem map. It is one of the most important conceptual bridges in the paper.

---

## Proven Claim P6: Sparse/full recurrence theorem

### Recommended statement

> **Theorem P6 (Trace recurrence under a two-layer window).** In any sealed sequence whose primitive public trace has a two-layer chronological window, the incremental primitive trace cost is the size of the active footprint in the previous two public interfaces. In the fully coupled endpoint this gives
>
> `mu_{n+1} = mu_n + mu_{n-1} + kappa_n + kappa_{n-1}`.

### Recommended constant-payload corollary

> **Corollary P6a (Shifted Fibonacci specialization).** If `kappa_n = c` is constant and the sequence is fully coupled, then `U_n = mu_n + 2c` satisfies the Fibonacci recurrence.

### Avoid saying

> Cubical foundations scale by Fibonacci recurrence.

### Say instead

> The fixed-calculus depth-two theorem yields a Fibonacci envelope for fully coupled constant-payload sequences.

---

## Proven Claim P7: Presentation invariance of the count

### Recommended statement

> **Theorem P7 (Refactoring invariance).** The cost `mu` is invariant under the paper's canonical presentation-equivalence generators: rebundling, currying/uncurrying, transparent aliases, shell splitting, and computational replacement of derived trace.

### Why it helps

This protects the recurrence theorem from the objection that the count is a source-level formatting artifact.

---

## Proven Claim P8: Mechanized internal theorem package

### Recommended statement

> **Theorem P8 (Mechanized theorem-facing model).** The accompanying Agda development formalizes the fixed candidate/obligation calculus, normalized trace accounting, horn-subsumption theorem for the chosen representation, lower-bound witnesses, and recurrence arithmetic. It does not formalize a parser or elaborator adequacy theorem for arbitrary Cubical Agda programs.

### Tone recommendation

Present the artifact as an **audit of the formal model**, not as proof of the entire conjectural program.

# A5. Replacement title and abstract

## Title 

> **Toward Coherence Depth Two for Cubical Sealed Extensions**

## Abstract rewrite


> We study the public integration trace created when a type-theoretic foundation is extended by a sealed, globally acting structural layer. The guiding conjecture is that, in adequate cubical foundations, primitive public structural trace has exact depth two: unary data are not enough because univalence creates genuine binary naturality obligations, while higher structural obligations are computable from binary boundary trace by cubical horn filling. This paper proves the conjectural picture for a fixed formal sealed-extension calculus. The calculus separates payload from public trace, exact realized obligations `Real_k(X)` from minimal public signatures counted by `mu`, and primitive trace fields from derived horn-computational fields. In this setting, higher marginal structural obligations are represented as horn-extension packages; Kan composition and filling make the above-binary factors contractible/derived; and a swap obstruction shows that depth one is insufficient. A separate recent-history theorem turns the binary arity bound into a two-layer chronological window. Consequently, under an explicit full-coupling hypothesis, primitive trace cost satisfies `mu_{n+1} = mu_n + mu_{n-1} + kappa_n + kappa_{n-1}`; under constant payload the shifted sequence is Fibonacci. The accompanying Agda development checks the fixed model theorem, recurrence arithmetic, lower-bound witnesses, and trace-accounting discipline, while leaving broad adequacy transfer to arbitrary cubical surface calculi as a conjectural next step.

# A9. Stronger lower-bound presentation

The binary lower bound is one of the most accessible parts of the paper. It should be moved earlier and explained more slowly.

## Current issue

The swap obstruction proves that unary objectwise data do not suffice to determine admissibility. That is strong. But if the paper phrases the lower bound as "primitive binary public trace is necessary," some readers may ask whether the example is an inadmissible candidate rather than an admissible extension with irreducible binary data.

## Recommended fix

State two versions:

### Version 1: Decision lower bound, already supported

> Unary trace is insufficient to decide admissibility of a sealed extension.

The swap obstruction proves this.

### Version 2: Public-field lower bound, stronger if available

> There exist admissible sealed extensions with identical unary trace but distinct binary public trace not reconstructible from unary data.

Add this only if it can be formally or convincingly established.

## Pedagogical explanation

Use the boolean example as the first real mathematical example in the paper:

```text
Old interface exports:
  2 : Type
  pswap : 2 = 2    induced by the equivalence swapping left and right

Candidate unary action:
  F2 : 2 -> 2
  F2(_) = left

Binary sealing asks:
  transport along pswap of F2 = F2

But transport along pswap conjugates by swap, so constant-left becomes constant-right.
Therefore the binary check fails.
```

Then add the positive control:

```text
If F2 = id, the binary check succeeds.
So the obstruction is not that the formulation is impossible; it is that unary data alone cannot tell whether the candidate is sealable.
```

This example will help non-specialist readers understand why depth one is not enough.

# Part B. Pedagogy, readability, and engagement

## B1. Make the paper's story linear

The current draft contains the right ingredients, but the reader encounters a dense vocabulary before seeing the simple phenomenon. Reorder the exposition so the story is:

1. **Sealing creates public trace debt.**
2. **Unary trace is not enough in univalent settings.**
3. **Binary trace handles naturality/comparison data.**
4. **Higher structural obligations appear as horn-extension packages.**
5. **Cubical filling computes these packages.**
6. **Therefore public primitive trace stabilizes at depth two in the fixed calculus.**
7. **If the extension is fully coupled to the last two layers, counting gives the recurrence.**

This sequence is intuitive. Build every section around it.

## B2. Introduce the running example before definitions

Use a running example with four versions, increasing in sophistication.

### Example 1: Old path, new action

Purpose: show unary vs binary trace.

```text
Old interface:
  A, B : Type
  p : A ~= B

New layer:
  F : Type -> Type

Unary trace:
  F_A : action of F on A
  F_B : action of F on B

Binary trace:
  F must respect p:
      F(p) agrees with the comparison between F_A and F_B.
```

This example makes binary trace feel unavoidable.

### Example 2: Boolean swap lower bound

Purpose: prove depth one is insufficient.

Use the swap obstruction early, with a diagram and one computation.

### Example 3: Old universe plus new modality

Purpose: show payload vs trace and horn-derived depth-three data.

A simplified version of the current `OldU`/`Flat` sketch should appear in the introduction or immediately after the first formal definitions, not buried deep in Section 5.

Recommended narrative:

- payload: `Flat` and `eta`;
- unary trace: `FlatU` and `flat-El`;
- binary trace: preservation of `Arr` and decoding comparisons;
- apparent depth-three trace: compatibility among the two routes through `El`, `Flat`, and `Arr`;
- cubical filling computes the apparent depth-three compatibility.

### Example 4: Recurrence table

Purpose: make the Fibonacci count tangible.

For constant payload `c`, show:

| stage | payload `kappa_n` | trace `mu_n` | total public increment `kappa_n + mu_n` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | `c` | `0` or seed-dependent | seed-dependent |
| 2 | `c` | `c` | `2c` |
| 3 | `c` | `3c` | `4c` |
| 4 | `c` | `6c` | `7c` |
| 5 | `c` | `11c` | `12c` |

Then say the exact seeds depend on the bootstrapping convention, but the shifted recurrence is Fibonacci.
